# Discovery and interviews

Use discovery to reduce consequential uncertainty, not to perform a fixed
ceremony. Ask one coherent question at a time, explain why the answer matters,
and summarize the answer for correction before relying on it.

## Discovery frame

Establish these facts from product authority, observed behaviour, and responsible
stakeholders:

| Area | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Problem | What happens today? Who experiences it? How often? What evidence shows the impact? |
| Outcome | What should become observably different? Who can judge success? By when, if time is material? |
| Actors | Who triggers, participates in, receives, administers, audits, or is harmed by the flow? |
| Current flow | What starts the flow, which states follow, where does it end, and what workarounds exist? |
| Rules | Which permissions, calculations, eligibility rules, deadlines, and state transitions must always hold? |
| Exceptions | What happens for invalid input, unavailable dependency, denied permission, duplicate action, timeout, partial completion, retry, and cancellation? |
| Scope | Which outcomes are included, excluded, or deferred? Why might a reader wrongly assume a non-goal is included? |
| Success | Which observable measure, artifact, or decision indicates the problem improved without unacceptable harm? |
| Authority | Who can approve scope, rules, thresholds, and trade-offs? Which questions remain outside the interviewee's authority? |

Do not ask every question when evidence already answers it. Do not omit a
material question merely to reduce question count.

## Techniques

### Five Whys

Use when the stated problem may be a symptom. Stop before speculation: each
answer needs evidence or a responsible source. The fifth why is not inherently
the root cause.

### Jobs to Be Done

Use when solution requests hide the outcome. Capture:

> When `<situation>`, the actor wants to `<progress>`, so they can `<outcome>`.

Separate functional progress from emotional or social outcomes when those
outcomes affect adoption or acceptance.

### SMART outcomes

Use Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound as a review lens,
not as permission to invent a target or deadline. Unknown measures and dates
remain owned open questions.

### Current/target flow comparison

For each flow record trigger, ordered actor actions, business states, decision
points, exceptions, and observable end state. Name what changes and what must
remain compatible.

## Interview discipline

- Interview stakeholders about their needs and evidence, not about facts outside
  their authority.
- Ask for concrete recent examples before general opinions.
- Distinguish frequency from severity and stated preference from observed work.
- Seek disconfirming cases: when does the problem not occur, and who benefits
  from the current behaviour?
- Record quotations only when wording itself matters; otherwise capture a
  verified summary.
- End with decisions, assumptions, open questions, owners, and follow-up
  evidence—not a transcript dump.

## Research discipline

Define the research question before searching. Record source, date, population,
method, and limitations. Competitive behaviour can reveal alternatives and
risks but cannot establish this product's requirement. Market data can support
priority proposals but cannot approve them.
